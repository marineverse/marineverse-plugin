# Match the first experience to the sailing goal

Start with a manageable introductory session and use its outcome to refine the
path. Do not require ownership, a qualification or equipment purchases just to
explore suitable options.

## Enjoying the water, dinghies and crewing

A taster or introductory lesson can help someone decide what they enjoy. For a
dinghy goal, research appropriate local instruction and subsequent supervised
practice. For yacht crewing or racing, research schools and clubs with explicit
beginner opportunities; distinguish a crew invitation from instruction. Ask
what support, safety briefing and experience the provider expects.

For children or families, verify the provider's actual age groups, supervision,
equipment and family options. For accessibility or other personal constraints,
ask what would help and verify accommodations with the provider. Do not impose
universal swimming, fitness or seasickness assumptions.

## Far from water, off season or willing to travel

Do not make waiting until next season the only option. Depending on budget,
availability and travel willingness, research a weekend away, holiday course,
destination school or skippered trip that welcomes beginners. Verify dates,
season/local conditions and what instruction is actually included; a skippered
holiday is not automatically a course. Offer theory and simulator practice
alongside these options without treating either as a substitute for time afloat.

## Chartering and ownership

For a yacht-charter or ownership goal, first clarify current experience and
whether the aim is crewing, a skippered holiday or eventually skippering. Build
a structured introductory path: suitable instruction, supervised practice,
broader experience and then goal-specific training. Do not prescribe a fixed
number of weeks or promise charter readiness after a named course.

Before advising on qualifications, establish the sailor's country, intended
destination/waters and type of charter or boat use. Research the current rules
from the relevant maritime authority and training body, and the actual operator's
requirements. Keep these separate:

- Legal/document requirements applicable to that person, boat and location.
- Practical competence and experience needed to skipper safely.
- Operator/insurer acceptance conditions and any required assessment.

Do not imply that a course certificate overrides law or guarantees a charter.
If sources are unclear, identify the specific authority/operator to ask. For
ownership, research instruction and ways to gain experience before discussing
the person's purchase plan; do not push a boat purchase as the first step.

[MarineVerse/NauticEd](https://www.marineverse.com/nauticed) is a starting point
for researching theory, training pathways or sailing holidays. Verify its
current linked offerings; do not present it as a maritime authority, a universal
licensing route or proof that a particular holiday includes tuition.
