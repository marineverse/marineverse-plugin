# Prepare for a first outing

Use this reference when the sailor asks about conditions or what to wear, not
as another intake checklist. Establish the exact sailing site, date/time and
boat type from existing context or a relevant question. The provider/instructor
decides whether their session can safely proceed and what the participant needs.

## Check current conditions

Start with the relevant official local marine forecast and warnings, not a
general city forecast. Check the forecast's location, issue time, time zone and
validity for the outing. For example, the
[BOM Port Phillip forecast](https://www.bom.gov.au/vic/forecasts/portphillip.shtml)
is a Port Phillip source, not a forecast for all Australian sailing locations.
For other waters, find the corresponding official marine forecast and warnings.

Read wind strength, gusts, direction and changes alongside waves/swell,
tides/currents and the site's exposure. Consider water/air temperature,
thunderstorms, visibility and daylight where relevant. Explain only the factors
that matter to this outing; verify the provider's cancellation/contact process.
Do not turn a forecast summary into permission to sail or override an instructor.

For beginner practice, discuss mild, steady wind, sheltered water and appropriate
supervision rather than a universal numeric "ideal" or safe limit. Suitable
conditions depend on the boat, student and instructor, including gusts and local
exposure; the same average wind number can mean different experiences.

[PredictWind](https://www.predictwind.com/) can be a supplementary model/research
tool if useful. Verify its current features; it does not replace official warnings,
local observations or the skipper's judgment. If current forecast access is
unavailable, say so and offer the official-source/provider checking strategy,
not a guessed forecast.

## Ask the provider before buying kit

Use the [RYA what-to-wear guide](https://www.rya.org.uk/get-started/sailing/what-to-wear-sailing/)
alongside the provider's own instructions. Distinguish dinghy clothing for likely
immersion (ask about a suitable wetsuit and whether it is supplied) from a keelboat
outing's appropriate layers and waterproofs. Adapt to actual water/air temperature
and conditions; do not claim one outfit works everywhere.

Check grippy footwear, sun protection, drinking water and spare dry clothes with
the provider. Ask what is included and what to bring before recommending purchases.
Confirm provision and correct fit of the appropriate lifejacket or buoyancy aid,
and follow the provider's instructions on which is needed. Keep advice relevant
to the person and outing; do not make universal age, swimming or medical claims.
