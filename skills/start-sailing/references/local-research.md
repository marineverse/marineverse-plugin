# Research a first local sailing opportunity

## Start with official guidance

Once the country and area are known, identify the country's sailing governing
body and its current beginner guidance, training-centre or club directory.
Follow its links to nearby providers; do not treat a national scheme as a legal
requirement or assume every listed club teaches beginners.

Useful starting points, to verify and follow at the time of the request:

- Australia: [Discover Sailing](https://www.discoversailing.org.au/) and
  [Australian Sailing course finder](https://www.sailing.org.au/course-finder/).
- United Kingdom: [Royal Yachting Association](https://www.rya.org.uk/).
- United States: [US Sailing](https://www.ussailing.org/); for another training
  provider network, [American Sailing schools](https://americansailing.com/find-a-school/).
- Poland: [Polski Związek Żeglarski](https://pya.org.pl/polski-zwiazek-zeglarski/).

These are research entry points, not an exhaustive country list. For another
country, find the corresponding official body rather than importing these
countries' pathways or inventing an authoritative directory.

## Check providers and current offerings

Match two or three useful candidates to the person's travel radius and goal.
Read the club/school's own current pages for introductory or taster sessions,
weekend or holiday courses, and beginner-friendly crewing or twilight sailing.
For a motivated beginner, a club's crew coordinator may be worth contacting;
confirm whether beginners are welcome rather than promising a berth.

Distinguish dinghy sailing from yacht sailing and instruction from social or
racing membership. Check adult/child/family suitability, season, published dates,
contact details and any relevant provider prerequisites. Mark missing dates or
unclear teaching availability as questions to ask, not evidence of availability.
Use the provider's published email/phone/contact page; never invent a contact.
Verify seasonal schedules rather than assuming a season from the hemisphere.
A course schedule is not today's weather or a safe-outing assessment; leave
weather suitability and go/no-go decisions to the provider or instructor.

Offer a short draft inquiry using known context: experience, goal, age group if
relevant, travel/availability, and whether they offer a suitable introductory
session or beginner crewing. Ask about equipment, prerequisites, next dates,
costs and what is included where not already published. Do not send it or equate
a draft with registration.

## MarineVerse directory and research limits

The sibling [clubs skill](../../marineverse-clubs/SKILL.md) covers optional
`search_groups` discovery and membership actions. Discover the host schema;
use `club_type: sailing_school` for a request specifically for schools. Use a
city query unless coordinates were supplied or resolved from a trusted source.
Retain returned canonical MarineVerse links for directory results, but use
providers' own pages to verify current real-world courses and contacts.

An empty or unmapped directory result is not an exhaustive local search. Continue
with official directories and provider websites if host web research is available.
Otherwise explain the limit and offer a concrete contact/research strategy with
unverified details clearly labelled, not fabricated local recommendations.
