# Start club racing

Use this for real-world club racing, not MarineVerse leaderboards. Reuse known
experience and location; ask one or two relevant questions about crew versus
helm, dinghy versus keelboat and access to a boat. For a beginner keen to race,
the preferred first route is joining an existing welcoming crew and learning
with them, matched to the sailor's goal and the skipper's expectations. An
alternative is a club Start Racing course where currently offered.

Research the club's current beginner crewing, welcome sessions, race training
or twilight offerings. Verify whether beginners are accepted and who coordinates
crew or training; do not assume every race welcomes them or that membership,
boat access or tuition is free. Distinguish being welcomed aboard from receiving
instruction. Offer a draft inquiry without sending it, joining or registering.

Ask the club about relevant entry requirements, boat/crew access, briefing and
appropriate flotation/safety equipment, fees and any applicable licence or
insurance conditions. Verify these for the particular event and role rather
than inventing a universal racing prerequisite. For a first outing, focus on
the skipper's briefing, local routine, start/course basics and a few relevant
rules rather than presenting a rulebook wall.

## Find the rules that actually apply

For an illustrated explanation or reconstruction of a situation, use the
[sailing-scenarios skill](../../sailing-scenarios/SKILL.md) to share a step-by-step
diagram. It supports discussion, not automated protest decisions.

Use [World Sailing's racing-rules page](https://www.sailing.org/racingrules)
to find the current edition, then the event's current Notice of Race and Sailing
Instructions, local prescriptions and class rules where applicable. Check their
scope rather than assuming a general summary covers the event.

[MarineVerse racing rules](https://www.marineverse.com/marineverse-cup/racing-rules)
is a simplified learning resource to inspect when available; verify its current
contents before explaining them. Avoid shortcuts such as "starboard always wins"
and do not present racing rules as replacing navigation rules or safe conduct.
For uncertainty, ask the club/instructor or consult the applicable official text.

MarineVerse Sailing Club practice can complement race-start and rules learning.
Where supported by the current platform, multiplayer can offer virtual practice
with other people before an actual race. Verify current features; do not promise
available partners or that virtual practice establishes real-world competence.
Read [MarineVerse practice](marineverse-practice.md) for available setups and the
existing [simulator racing reference](../../marineverse-mcp/references/racing.md)
only for MarineVerse results, events and supported practice tools. Simulator
race availability or a read of results is not entry to a real-world club race.
