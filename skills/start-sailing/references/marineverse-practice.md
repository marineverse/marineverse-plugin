# Use MarineVerse around real lessons

Connect practice to the person's next on-water experience: wind direction and
sailing angles before a first lesson, repetition between outings, and revisiting
an instructor's feedback afterwards. Keep the plan small and relevant. VR is
the preferred immersive MarineVerse experience, not a requirement for being
serious about sailing or a replacement for real instruction.

## Pick a resource for the practice goal

- [Points of Sail Trainer](https://www.marineverse.com/points-of-sail-trainer):
  explore wind angles and sail trim before revisiting them on the water.
- [Try Sailing](https://www.marineverse.com/try-sailing/): interactive browser
  practice with wind basics, without a headset purchase.
- [Learn how to sail](https://www.marineverse.com/learn-how-to-sail): find relevant
  learning resources rather than presenting the whole catalogue.
- [Beaufort scale](https://www.marineverse.com/beaufort-scale): an educational
  wind-strength demonstration, not a forecast or a safe-outing decision tool.

## Choose the available setup

Before recommending a hardware purchase, ask whether they already have a headset
and which model. For SteamVR, establish the headset and PC setup; do not assume
that every headset runs every MarineVerse app. Research current official app
support, store requirements and any trial offer for their exact setup. Check
age/family suitability and platform restrictions when relevant, not from a fixed
age or compatibility promise.

If their headset is compatible, direct them to the current official app or trial
information before discussing new hardware. If they have no headset, compatibility
is uncertain, or they prefer a browser, offer
[Try Sailing](https://www.marineverse.com/try-sailing/) for exploring wind basics
without making a headset purchase a prerequisite.

If they specifically want to invest in regular VR practice, consider Quest 3
as a candidate, subject to current MarineVerse support, hardware availability,
local pricing and their constraints. Research those facts at the time; do not
recommend buying it simply because they have a serious real-world sailing goal.
Respect a browser, desktop or existing-hardware preference.

## Theory and public tools

Use [MarineVerse/NauticEd](https://www.marineverse.com/nauticed) to research current
theory, pathway and holiday options alongside real lessons. Explain only what
the current official pages support; do not promise accreditation, a free trial,
pricing or qualification acceptance without verification.

MarineVerse tool names are optional and host-dependent: discover schemas first.
`get_marineverse_links` can return official practice destinations. Public glossary
lookup/search can support an unfamiliar term; the existing
[public-content reference](../../marineverse-mcp/references/public-content.md)
covers `get_sailing_term` and `list_sailing_terms`. Their published definitions
are sources, not permission to take actions.

`search_public_content` searches MarineVerse product FAQs and release history,
not the general web or local course availability. Free beginner guidance does
not require paid knowledge-base search, OAuth or CLI installation. If host web
research or public tools are unavailable, offer the known entry-point links and
say current support, trials, prices and offerings have not been verified.
