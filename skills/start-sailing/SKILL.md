# Start sailing

Help the sailor take a practical first step on the water, supported by theory
and MarineVerse practice. Research their current situation rather than giving
a fixed country, course or equipment recommendation. No MarineVerse account,
CLI installation or headset is required for this guidance.

## Start with the person

Reuse what they have already told you. Ask one or two relevant questions at a
time, not an intake survey. Usually begin with their country/town and previous
experience; then establish the goal and what would make a first outing feasible.
Discover proximity to water, travel willingness, season and local conditions,
and constraints such as time, budget or accessibility as they become relevant.
Ask whether this is for an adult, child or family when it affects the options.

Distinguish wanting to enjoy the water or try a dinghy, crew on a yacht, race,
own a boat or charter a yacht. A long-term dream is useful context, not a reason
to skip introductory instruction or prescribe a qualification immediately.

Present a small set of relevant, sourced options and an achievable next action.
Distinguish a club from a school and a directory listing from a confirmed teaching
offer. Offer to draft an inquiry email or call script; do not send, book or join.
Adapt after the sailor answers rather than repeating the same questions.

## Evidence and boundaries

Use host web research when available to verify current offerings, contacts,
hardware, prices and applicable requirements. Cite the actual supporting pages.
If research is unavailable, say which facts remain unverified and offer an
official-source/contact strategy; never pretend a search occurred. MarineVerse
public content search is not general web search, and an empty MarineVerse club
directory result does not establish that no local sailing exists.

Separate legal requirements, practical competence and school/charter-operator
conditions. Do not make blanket claims about licences, swimming, seasickness,
cost or learning time. Simulator practice does not certify sailing ability or
passage readiness; real instruction and local conditions still matter.
